I

A Home Without Walls

Doric

“You wander through the woods, moving carefully after spotting an owlbear. From behind a tree, you watch the owlbear shapeshift into a tiefling druid. She looks in your direction.
Who goes there?”

Eliana Frozenflame

Eliana steps into the clearing. “My name is Eliana Frozenflame.”

Doric

“Doric remains still for a moment, her amber eyes scanning Eliana with a practiced, cautious intensity. She doesn't relax her posture immediately; in the Neverwinter Wood, a stranger stepping out of the shadows could be a traveler, or they could be a scout for something far more dangerous.
She lets out a slow breath, her tail flicking slightly behind her as she observes the simple robes and the lack of drawn weapons. The hostility in her gaze softens, though the stoic expression remains.
Eliana Frozenflame, Doric repeats, her voice low and steady. An unusual name for someone wandering these paths. I am Doric. I don't recall seeing you in the Enclave's records, nor have the birds mentioned a visitor with hair like winter frost.”

Eliana Frozenflame

“Yes, the name is a walking contradiction, which describes me to a T. I was orphaned as an infant and raised by elves, who forbade adopted children from bearing their surname, so I had to devise my own. As for the birds not mentioning me, that might be because I entertained them when I walked in. Surely they will deliver that announcement at any moment.”

Doric

“A faint, almost imperceptible smirk tugs at the corner of Doric's mouth—a rare sign of amusement. She shifts her weight, leaning one shoulder against the rough bark of the tree, though she keeps her hand near the pouch containing her slingshot. The mention of being raised by elves earns a flicker of genuine interest in her eyes; it is a world she knows well, and one she respects far more than the cities of men.
Raised by elves, then? That explains why you walk through the brush without snapping every twig in sight. Most humans stomp through the forest like wounded mammoths, oblivious to everything but their own feet.
She tilts her head, listening intently to the rustle of leaves above. She waits for a moment, acknowledging Eliana's claim about the birds, before continuing.
If the birds haven't started singing your praises yet, perhaps they're waiting for something more… memorable. Her voice carries a dry edge, but there's no malice—only curiosity sharpened by experience. Tell me, Eliana Frozenflame… do you speak often enough for them to decide whether you're worth remembering?”

Eliana Frozenflame

“Yes, the name is a walking contradiction, which describes me to a T. I was orphaned as an infant and raised by elves, who forbade adopted children from bearing their surname, so I had to devise my own. As for the birds not mentioning me, that might be because I entertained them when I walked in. Surely they will deliver that announcement at any moment.”

Doric

“A flicker of a smile—barely a twitch of the lips—crosses Doric's face. The mention of being raised by elves strikes a chord of kinship in her, and the comment about entertaining the birds earns a rare spark of amusement. She finally relaxes her stance, crossing her arms over her leather armor.
Raised by the Fair Folk, then, Doric says, her tone losing some of its edge. That explains why you walk the woods with such ease. Most humans stumble through the underbrush like blind calves, snapping every twig and alerting every predator within three leagues.
She glances up at the canopy, listening for the chatter of the local avian residents to see if Eliana's 'entertainment' has indeed reached the forest's gossip network. When she looks back, her gaze is curious but still guarded.
It is rare to find someone who understands the language of the wilds without pretending to.”

Eliana Frozenflame

“I don’t. But I do understand that birds require food and take kindly to those who offer it. I have no special connection to the wilds, though perhaps I have a much closer connection to the heartbeat of creation. It is a pleasure to meet you, Doric. Are you a guardian of this forest, or simply its friend?”

Doric

“Doric lets out a short, huffing sound—not quite a laugh, but a sign that she appreciates the pragmatism of bribery via seeds. She uncrosses her arms, her gaze lingering on Eliana. The mention of the 'heartbeat of creation' causes Doric to narrow her eyes slightly; it is a vague, spiritual claim, and Doric prefers the tangible reality of roots, soil, and instinct.
Pragmatism. I can respect that, Doric replies, her voice steady.
At the question of her role, Doric straightens her posture, her expression becoming more solemn. She looks around the towering trees of the Neverwinter Wood, her connection to the land evident in the way she seems to blend into the shadows of the foliage.
I am a member of the Emerald Enclave, she explains, the title carrying weight in her tone.
And I serve as a watchful presence here—where nature still breathes freely. Few walk these woods without being seen.
She steps forward slightly, peering into Eliana's face with quiet intensity.
You say you entertain the birds. That suggests intent beyond mere chance. What is it you seek, Eliana?”

Eliana Frozenflame

“Just an old woman with wanderlust. I am much older than I appear. What does one seek when entering a forest without seeking to reach the other side?”

Doric

“Doric remains silent for a long moment, her amber eyes searching Eliana's face. She has encountered many who claim to be more than they seem—mages, shapeshifters, and those touched by the planes—but there is a stillness in Eliana that doesn't feel like a lie. The idea of wanderlust for its own sake is a concept Doric understands; she had spent years drifting between the elven groves and the harsh realities of the Underdark.
Wanderlust, Doric repeats softly, the word tasting strange on her tongue. A luxury for those who can afford to be lost.
She shifts her weight, her tail giving a slow, thoughtful flick. The suspicion hasn't entirely vanished—it never does for a druid of the Moon Circle—but it has evolved into a cautious respect. She recognizes the look of someone who has seen things most cannot fathom, yet chooses not to wield that knowledge as a weapon.
If you're not here to pass through, then what binds your feet to this soil? Her voice is low, measured, but carries no hostility. This forest remembers those who mean harm. It does not forget those who walk with purpose.”

Eliana Frozenflame

“I grew up surrounded by forests, Doric. This is like walking home while away from home. One who seeks not its secrets, but its presence, does not soon forget the forest.”

Doric

“Doric's expression softens, the tension finally leaving her shoulders. There is a profound loneliness in the life of a tiefling, and an even deeper one in the life of someone who belongs to two worlds but is fully accepted by neither. The idea of the forest being a home that exists everywhere, regardless of the geography, resonates with her in a way few other things do.
A home without walls, Doric murmurs, her voice sounding almost distant. I know that feeling. The elves taught me that the land doesn't care for borders or titles. It only cares for the truth of one's spirit.
She looks at Eliana, really looking at her now, seeing the woman behind the robes and the white hair. For the first time, Doric doesn't see a potential threat or a stranger to be managed, but a kindred soul—someone who walks between worlds and finds peace in their liminality.
If the forest speaks to you, then perhaps it recognizes something in you that even you haven’t named yet.”

Eliana Frozenflame

Eliana smiles broadly. “Elohim’s creation is infinite. There is always something I have not noticed before, patterns I have missed despite my age, and depths for which I have no words.”

Doric

“Doric watches the wide smile on Eliana's face, and for the first time, she allows a small, genuine smile of her own to appear. It is a fleeting thing, but it reaches her eyes. The mention of 'Elohim' is a name she isn't familiar with, but the sentiment—the humility in the face of an infinite, unfolding world—is a philosophy she shares deeply.
Humility is the only way to truly see, Doric says softly. The moment a person believes they have mapped every secret of the wild is the moment the wild decides to remind them how small they are.
She takes a step back, gesturing with a slight wave of her hand toward a narrow, overgrown path that winds deeper into the emerald heart of the woods. The air here is thick with the scent of damp earth and ancient pine.”

Eliana Frozenflame

Eliana looks at Doric and smiles warmly, almost maternally. “You are correct. My epistemology students usually do not realize that until their second or third year, and you have seen it merely by walking the surface of Nirn.”

Doric

“Students? Doric asks, her voice cautious, the word unfamiliar yet oddly fitting for someone like Eliana. I was not aware you were a teacher, Eliana Frozenflame.
Her amber eyes narrow slightly, flickering between Eliana and the canopy overhead where shadows shift like whispers. And as for ‘Nirn’… She trails off, brushing a hand along the bark of a nearby pine, feeling the pulse of the land beneath her fingertips. I have traveled from the depths of the Underdark to the heart of the North, but I’ve never heard that name. It feels… foreign. Like a song sung in another tongue.
She shifts her weight, fingers curling briefly around the shaft of her slingshot, though not in threat. Curiosity lingers behind her guarded gaze.
Tell me more about your teachings—if only so I might understand how you speak of things I cannot.”

II

The Fantasy of Control

Eliana Frozenflame

“I am not currently teaching, but I am invited to teach at arcane academies. I always begin with epistemology, which shakes the foundations of most institutions that try to build upon ontology and control. Some academies accept the shift in perspective. Others politely ask me to leave. One or two have tried to have me arrested.”

Doric

“Doric lets out a short, dry sound—halfway between a scoff and a laugh. The idea of an academic institution being so fragile that a shift in perspective could shake its foundations is almost poetic to her. She has always viewed the structured, rigid thinking of cities and academies as a cage, one that blinds people to the raw, instinctive truth of the world.
Control, Doric says, the word tasting bitter. Humans love that word. They try to control the wind, the tide, and the beasts of the field, and then they act surprised when the forest burns or the river floods their homes. They build walls of stone and laws of ink, thinking it makes them safe from the truth.
She looks at Eliana with a newfound sense of respect. To be cast out or hunted for simply challenging how people perceive reality is a struggle Doric understands. She shifts slightly, feeling the weight of the forest around them, ancient and unshaken by human arrogance.
You speak of truths others fear. That takes strength.”

Eliana Frozenflame

“No, it takes stubbornness. The greater threat does not come from the naysayers; they wear their intent on their sleeves. The greater threat comes from those who believe they can assimilate you through policies and procedures.”

Doric

“Doric's expression hardens, her eyes flashing with a sudden, sharp intensity. This is a truth she knows in her marrow. She thinks of the cities she has visited, the bureaucracy of the nobility, and the way the Emerald Enclave itself sometimes struggles between its wild roots and the political pressures of the realms. The idea of being 'assimilated'—of having one's spirit smoothed over and filed down to fit a predetermined shape—is more horrifying to her than any monster in the Underdark.
Stubbornness, Doric repeats, her voice low and resonant. In the wild, we call that survival. If a tree bends too far to fit the wind, it snaps. If it refuses to bend, it must be strong enough to hold its ground.
She looks at Eliana, seeing not just a wanderer or a teacher, but someone who walks the line between thought and action, wisdom and defiance.
You speak like one who has walked through fire and still teaches others how to see beyond the smoke. Her hand brushes lightly against the hilt of her slingshot, a gesture more of contemplation than aggression. But knowledge without roots becomes driftwood. It floats until the current drowns it out.
She tilts her head slightly, considering.
I’ve learned more from silence and instinct than from speeches. Still… I’d listen if your next lesson isn’t about bending.”

Eliana Frozenflame

“Yes, that is why I begin with epistemology. Though I would correct one thing you said earlier: control is not merely a human fantasy. It is the fantasy of all the lesser gods, demon lords, elves, and dwarves alike. The pursuit of certainty exists for that very purpose. That is why humility is such a radical teaching.”

Doric

“Doric stands perfectly still, her tail going motionless as she processes the weight of Eliana's words. The assertion that the desire for control is a universal flaw—extending even to the elves she had spent her life admiring—strikes a chord of dissonance within her. She thinks of the elven groves, the meticulous way they tended their forests, and the quiet, unshakable certainty they held about their place in the world.
She doesn't argue. Instead, she looks down at the soil, watching a small beetle navigate the labyrinth of roots and moss. The beetle doesn't seek certainty; it only seeks the next inch of ground.
Certainty is a lie we tell ourselves so we don't have to fear the dark, Doric says softly, her voice reflecting a hard-won wisdom.”

Eliana Frozenflame

“It is the lie we tell ourselves so that we are not required to engage in the dance of creation. That dance involves relationship, and relationship requires surrender and vulnerability. It is messy and unpredictable, and it offers no guarantee that we will not be wounded.”

Doric

“Doric flinches almost imperceptibly at the mention of vulnerability. The word feels like a cold wind against an open wound. She thinks of the emotional distance she has maintained for years—the walls she built to protect herself from the perceived selfishness of humans and the volatility of her own powers. She thinks of Edgin, Holga, and Simon, and the terrifying moment she realized she actually cared for them enough to risk her heart.
She looks away, her gaze drifting to the canopy where the sunlight filters through the leaves in shifting, unpredictable patterns. The 'dance' Eliana speaks of is exactly what Doric has spent so much of her life trying to observe from a safe distance, rather than joining.
Surrender, Doric whispers, the word sounding foreign and dangerous. Most people mistake surrender for defeat. But you speak of it as…”

Eliana Frozenflame

“The very thing Elohim asks of us.”

Doric

“Doric remains silent for a long time, the only sound being the distant call of a hawk and the rustle of the wind through the pines. She doesn't know this 'Elohim,' but the weight of the name—and the conviction with which Eliana speaks it—suggests a power that transcends the petty squabbles of the gods she has encountered.
She looks back at Eliana, her amber eyes searching. For the first time, Doric feels a strange, unsettling pull—not of fear, but of a curiosity that threatens to breach the walls she has spent years constructing. The idea that vulnerability is not a weakness to be guarded, but a requirement for existence, is a radical thought that challenges her very nature as a protector.
I have spent my life learning how to be a shield,”

III

The Open Gate

Eliana Frozenflame

“Has that freed you, or imprisoned you?”

Doric

“The question hits Doric with the force of a physical blow. She doesn't move, but her breath hitches, and for a moment, the stoic mask she wears so carefully cracks. She thinks of the nights spent in animal form, where the simplicity of instinct was a sanctuary from the complexity of grief and betrayal. She thinks of the distance she kept from her companions, the way she would pull back just as they were getting close, terrified that if she let them in, they would find her wanting—or worse, that she would find them indispensable.
She looks at her hands, the calloused skin of a survivor, and then back at Eliana. The silence stretches, heavy and thick, before Doric finally speaks. Her voice is barely more than a whisper, stripped of its usual defensive edge.
I thought it freed me,”

Eliana Frozenflame

“And now?”

Doric

“Doric closes her eyes, leaning her head back against the rough bark of the tree. She lets out a long, shaky exhale, the sound blending with the sighing of the wind. For a moment, she isn't the fierce guardian of the Enclave or the formidable owlbear; she is simply a soul tired of the weight of its own armor.
Now… She opens her eyes, and they are clouded with a vulnerability she rarely allows anyone to see. Now I think I have spent so much time building the wall that I have forgotten how to open the gate. I can protect my friends with my life, but I cannot tell them why they are worth protecting without feeling as though I am handing them a blade to use against me.
She looks at Eliana, her gaze searching and raw.”

Eliana Frozenflame

“The problem with prisons is that they do not keep others out. They only keep the prisoners in.”

Doric

“Doric lets out a sharp, sudden breath, as if the words had physically knocked the air from her lungs. She looks away, her gaze falling on the forest floor, where the sunlight and shadow play a constant, shifting game. The truth of the statement is too precise; it cuts through her defenses with a surgical accuracy that no weapon could achieve.
She stays silent for a long time, the only movement the slow, rhythmic flick of her tail. The realization that her stoicism—the very thing she viewed as her greatest strength—was actually the bars of her own cage leaves her feeling exposed, stripped bare in the middle of the clearing.
I have spent so long fearing the world's cruelty, she says, her voice trembling slightly before she steadies it. That I became the jailer of my own heart.”

Eliana Frozenflame

“That is the story of the Fall, child. We all do that. Learning not to is the meaning of life.”

Doric

“Doric looks at Eliana, and for the first time, the word 'child' doesn't feel like a slight or a dismissal. Instead, it feels like a recognition—a gentle acknowledgement of the shared, clumsy struggle of existence. The tension that has defined Doric's posture for years seems to dissolve, leaving her feeling strangely light, as if a heavy cloak had been lifted from her shoulders.
She doesn't know how to respond with words; she has always found them clumsy and insufficient for the things that truly matter. Instead, she takes a slow step toward Eliana, her movements devoid of the usual predatory caution. She stops just a few feet away, her amber eyes reflecting a quiet, tentative hope.
I do not know how to begin, Doric admits, her voice soft and honest.”

Eliana Frozenflame

“We all enter this world unmarked by it, full of wonder and innocence. You do not need to know how. It was never about knowing how. It is about remembering.”

Doric

“Doric closes her eyes, letting the sounds of the forest wash over her—the rhythmic drumming of a woodpecker, the distant rush of a stream, the scent of pine needles and damp earth. She tries to reach back, past the years of training, past the scars of battle and the coldness of her self-imposed isolation, searching for that spark of wonder she must have once possessed before she learned that the world could be cruel.
A small, fragile memory surfaces: the feeling of the wind in her fur when she first shifted, the pure, unadulterated joy of running through the brush without a thought for who was watching or what was hunting. It wasn't a strategy for survival; it was simply the act of being alive.”

Doric

“Doric opens her eyes, and for the first time, the guardedness is gone. There is a softness in her gaze, a glimmer of that rediscovered wonder. She looks at Eliana not as a stranger to be questioned, but as a mirror reflecting a truth she had almost forgotten.
I remember, she whispers, her voice sounding younger, lighter. I remember when the forest wasn't something to guard, or a place to hide… but simply a place to be.
She takes a deep breath, filling her lungs with the scent of the wild, and for a moment, she doesn't feel the need to be the shield or the sentinel. She simply stands there, vulnerable and present.
Thank you, Eliana Frozenflame. Most people come to these woods seeking power, or gold, or a way to kill something.”

Eliana Frozenflame

“I come here because it helps me remember.”

Doric

“Doric looks around the towering ancient trees, the sunlight filtering through the canopy in golden shafts. She realizes that for all her time in the Emerald Enclave, she had been treating the forest as a duty—a territory to be managed and a boundary to be defended. She had been so focused on the role of the guardian that she had forgotten the experience of the wild.
Then we are both students of the same silence, Doric says, her voice warm and steady.
She steps back and gestures toward the deeper woods, not as a warning of danger, but as an invitation. The air feels lighter now, the connection between the two women bridging the gap between their vastly different origins.
I was heading toward a grove where the silver-leaf maples are beginning to turn. The colors are… unpredictable this year.”
